Most airlines already price ancillaries in-house. This lab is not another dashboard that only predicts a price. It shows how a revenue-management lead would evaluate a New Model against Current Pricing, apply Business Rules as the final control, introduce the model in a traffic ladder (shadow → 5% → 20% → 50% → 100%), measure commercial impact, and Return to Current Pricing if it is not healthy.

Revenue Management sets traffic, price bands, and temporary caps. This is not per-passenger approval. The New Model never bypasses Business Rules.

3. Pricing Test Results

We measure whether the New Model actually increases revenue versus Current Pricing without damaging conversion. n=222 chargeable bookings out of 500 simulated. Live New Model arm n=7. Not a live airline A/B test.

Current PricingNew Model
Revenue per chargeable booking (USD)USD 94.20USD 102.91

How to read the table: both columns cover the same bookings, the ones where a price is actually charged. A booking whose item is included in the fare, complimentary for the loyalty tier or out of inventory is free or unavailable whichever system prices it, so it is left out of both columns and counted below. The figures are expected values, using the New Model's estimated purchase chance at each price: Current Pricing at its own quote, the New Model at its price after Business Rules. Revenue per chargeable booking is the purchase rate multiplied by the average price paid.

Revenue impact: +9.2%   Conversion impact: +22.6 percentage points   Business-rule violations: 0

Left out of both columns, free or unavailable: 278 (231 included in the fare, 36 loyalty complimentary, 11 no inventory)

On the 222 chargeable bookings, the New Model increases expected revenue by 9.2% in the simulation while conversion moves +22.6 percentage points versus Current Pricing.

What this does not show: the +9.2% is the New Model's own estimate, not an observed result. All of the gain comes from extra baggage (+55.2%, n=75), which is in shadow on every route, so no customer has been shown a New Model baggage price. Extra-legroom is −1.3% (n=147). Only 7 bookings were served the New Model price, all on SIN–LHR extra-legroom, too few to read on their own.

Corrected 16 November 2026. Until then this page reported −44.3% revenue, −19.8 percentage points of conversion and a decision to Return to Current Pricing. That comparison was not like-for-like: it applied Business Rules to the New Model only, so on the 278 free or unavailable bookings the New Model counted as USD 0 while Current Pricing was still credited with its quote.
